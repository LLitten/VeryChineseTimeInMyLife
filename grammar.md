# Grammar patterns and practice sentences

Built from the learner's handwritten notes, then corrected where a note was incomplete.

- **Example sentences are written by Claude** unless they appear under *Practice sentences*, which come from the learner's notes.
- **⚑** marks a pattern where the learner's note needed a correction or addition. The correction is stated.

## Patterns

### 1. Subject + Time + Place + Verb + Object
- **Rule:** Basic order. Time words go before the verb.
- **Example:** 我明天在家吃饭。 I’ll eat at home tomorrow.

### 2. Time can open the sentence
- **Rule:** Time words can come first or right after the subject.
- **Example:** 明天我吃饭。 / 我明天吃饭。

### 3. … Manner + Place + Tool + With whom + Verb + Object
- **Rule:** The learner's long-sentence guideline. The middle pieces can shift in real speech.
- **Example:** Think: time, then place, then tool, then the action.
- ⚑ **Correction:** see the rule above; the learner's original note was incomplete, unclear or slightly off.

### 4. Verb + Duration + Object
- **Rule:** How long: duration goes right after the verb, before the object. The learner's notes put it after the object.
- **Example:** 我学了两个小时数学。 I studied math for two hours.
- ⚑ **Correction:** see the rule above; the learner's original note was incomplete, unclear or slightly off.

### 5. Verbs that take a place after them
- **Rule:** 住, 走, 坐 and a few others.
- **Example:** 我住在北京。 I live in Beijing. (坐 takes a vehicle: 坐飞机.)

### 6. Statement + 吗？
- **Rule:** Yes/no question; word order stays.
- **Example:** 你喝水吗？ Do you drink water?

### 7. Verb + 不 + Verb
- **Rule:** A-not-A question. Can’t be combined with 吗.
- **Example:** 你是不是学生？ 你喜不喜欢？

### 8. 有没有
- **Rule:** Have or don’t have. Use 有没有, not 有不有.
- **Example:** 你有没有书？ Do you have a book?

### 9. …，好不好？
- **Rule:** Tag question seeking agreement (“okay?”). Can’t use 没.
- **Example:** 我们明天吃饭，好不好？

### 10. Answering yes/no questions
- **Rule:** Yes: repeat the verb. No: negate the verb.
- **Example:** 你喝水吗？ 喝。 / 不喝。

### 11. 不 + Verb / Adjective
- **Rule:** Not: present, future, habits, unwillingness.
- **Example:** 我不吃鱼。 I don’t eat fish.

### 12. 没(有) + Verb
- **Rule:** Didn’t / haven’t yet. Never add 了.
- **Example:** 我昨天没吃饭。 I didn’t eat yesterday.

### 13. 别 / 不要 + Verb (+ 了)
- **Rule:** Don’t…; add 了 to mean “stop doing it.”
- **Example:** 别说了。 Stop talking.

### 14. 不太 + Adjective
- **Rule:** Not very. No 了 at the end.
- **Example:** 我不太高兴。 I’m not very happy.

### 15. 太 + Adjective + 了
- **Rule:** Too / so …! 了 is required.
- **Example:** 太好了！ Great!

### 16. A + 没有 + B + Adjective
- **Rule:** A is not as … as B.
- **Example:** 你没有我高。 You’re not as tall as me.

### 17. 很 + Adjective
- **Rule:** Adjectives already include “to be,” so skip 是.
- **Example:** 他很高。 He is tall.

### 18. A 和 B
- **Rule:** Joins nouns only.
- **Example:** 我和朋友 (my friend and I)

### 19. Subject + 也 + Verb
- **Rule:** Also. After the subject, never at the end.
- **Example:** 我也吃饭。 I also eat.

### 20. Subject + 都 + Verb
- **Rule:** All / both. Before the verb or adjective.
- **Example:** 我们都说英语。 We all speak English.

### 21. Subject + 会 + Verb
- **Rule:** Can (a learned skill). Also “will.”
- **Example:** 我会说中文。 I can speak Chinese. 明天会很好。 Tomorrow will be good.
- ⚑ **Correction:** see the rule above; the learner's original note was incomplete, unclear or slightly off.

### 22. Subject + 喜欢 + Verb
- **Rule:** Like doing something.
- **Example:** 我喜欢吃饭。

### 23. Subject + 想 + Verb
- **Rule:** Want to.
- **Example:** 我想买水。 I want to buy water.

### 24. Subject + 让 + Person + Verb
- **Rule:** Let, allow, make or ask someone to.
- **Example:** 他让我睡觉。 He lets me sleep.

### 25. Subject + 想让 + Person + Verb
- **Rule:** Want someone to.
- **Example:** 我想让你学习。 I want you to study.

### 26. 得 + Verb / 不用 + Verb
- **Rule:** Have to / don’t need to. Not 不得.
- **Example:** 我得吃饭。 / 我不用吃饭。

### 27. 还是 (questions) / 或者 (statements)
- **Rule:** Or.
- **Example:** 你想喝咖啡还是茶？ 我想喝咖啡或者茶。

### 28. 在 + Verb (+ 呢)
- **Rule:** In progress, “is doing.” 正在 stresses “right now.”
- **Example:** 她们在买衣服呢。 They’re buying clothes.

### 29. Place + 有 + Noun
- **Rule:** There is / there are.
- **Example:** 桌子上有一个杯子。 On the table there is a cup.

### 30. Verb + 到
- **Rule:** Result complement: you succeeded (找到, 听到, 看到, 买到).
- **Example:** 我找不到。 I can’t find it.

### 31. Verb + 来 / 去
- **Rule:** Toward (来) or away from (去) the speaker.
- **Example:** 下来 come down / 下去 go down

### 32. Question word stays in place
- **Rule:** The question word replaces the missing info; the sentence order is unchanged.
- **Example:** 你喜欢什么菜？ What food do you like?

### 33. Dates and times: big to small
- **Rule:** Year, month, day. Hour, minute. Addresses too.
- **Example:** 今天几月几号？ 十点 / 两点

### 34. Noun order
- **Rule:** Possessive, then this/that/number/measure, then adjective, then noun.
- **Example:** 我的两本书 (my two books)

### 35. Description + 的 + Noun
- **Rule:** Drop the noun to mean “the … one.”
- **Example:** 我的书 / 好的

### 36. Adjective + 地 + Verb
- **Rule:** Makes an adverb (like -ly). Not super common; plain adverbs don’t need it.
- **Example:** 清楚地说 / 清清楚楚地说

### 37. “It” is often dropped
- **Rule:** Say what “it” is directly.
- **Example:** 现在五点。 It’s 5 o’clock.

### 38. Adverbs go before the verb
- **Rule:** They can’t stand alone, except 不, 没有, 也许, 可能. A few (也许, 可能) can open a sentence.
- **Example:** 我很喜欢这本书。
- ⚑ **Correction:** see the rule above; the learner's original note was incomplete, unclear or slightly off.

## Corrections made to the learner's notes

- **Duration:** the notes put the duration after the object. The usual order is verb + duration + object: 我学了两个小时数学.
- **会:** the notes list only "can (a learned skill)." It also means "will" (明天会很好).
- **什么时候:** the notes label it "future." It also works for the past.
- **Adverbs:** the notes say adverbs can't come before the subject and also that some can open a sentence. Words like 也许 and 可能 can open a sentence.
- **不太:** pronounced bú tài (4th-tone change), and it takes no 了.
- **Long-sentence order** (time, manner, place, tool, companion, verb): treat it as a guideline. The middle pieces shift in real speech.
